## 1. Liveness Detection Vulnerabilities

### 1.1 Background

The system employs **MiniFASNet** (Silent-Face Anti-Spoofing, v2) as its passive liveness detection module. MiniFASNet is a lightweight, single-frame convolutional neural network trained to discriminate real faces from printed photos and replay attacks using Fourier Spectrum and feature-map supervision.

### 1.2 Known Attack Vectors

| Attack Type | Bypass Likelihood | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Printed photograph (flat) | Low | Effectively mitigated by MiniFASNet |
| Screen replay (phone/tablet) | Low–Medium | Depends on screen quality and ambient lighting |
| 3D printed silicone mask | **High** | Texture and depth cues closely mimic live skin |
| High-quality deepfake video | **High** | Temporal and spectral artefacts suppressed |
| Infrared spoof mask | Medium–High | IR channel not analysed by RGB-only model |

### 1.3 Analysis

MiniFASNet was trained predominantly on **CASIA-SURF**, **SiW**, and similar datasets that do not include photorealistic silicone masks or current-generation generative model (GAN/diffusion) deepfakes. As a result:

- **3D printed masks** exhibit a skin-like surface texture and 3-D geometry that MiniFASNet cannot reliably distinguish from genuine faces. Published studies (Liu et al., 2022; Tolosana et al., 2023) demonstrate that passive single-frame anti-spoofing models fail against high-fidelity physical artefacts.
- **High-quality deepfakes** generated by latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion + face swap pipelines) or StyleGAN3-based face generators exhibit spectral statistics close to real camera footage, causing passive anti-spoofing models trained before 2023 to misclassify them at rates exceeding 40 % in controlled experiments.

### 1.4 Mitigation Recommendations

- Integrate challenge-response liveness (randomised blink/turn prompts) for high-security deployments.
- Supplement with an IR or depth sensor (e.g., Intel RealSense D435) which is effective against flat prints and many 3-D mask attacks.
- Periodically re-train or fine-tune MiniFASNet on updated spoof datasets.

## 2. Mask and Occlusion Handling

### 2.1 Behaviour

The SCRFD face detector is capable of detecting masked faces; however, the ArcFace embedding network was not specifically trained on masked-face data. When more than approximately 30 % of the lower face is occluded:

- Embedding quality degrades significantly.
- Cosine distance to enrolled embeddings rises above the confirmation threshold.
- The system routes the detection to the **Unknown** category.

No partial-face recognition sub-module (e.g., MaskInsightFace, partial FC) is integrated in the current implementation.

### 2.2 Impact

In post-pandemic educational environments, medical or fabric face masks are common. The current system will fail to recognise masked students and will not mark attendance for them, requiring manual override via the Streamlit review queue.

### 2.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Integrate a masked-face ArcFace variant (e.g., ArcFace with partial FC trained on MFR2 dataset).
- Add an explicit "masked detection" flag and prompt the student to lower the mask momentarily for a single-frame capture.

## 3. Appearance Change and Re-enrollment

### 3.1 Behaviour

The system stores a fixed embedding vector obtained at enrollment time. No online embedding update or multiple-shot averaging across different days is performed. As a result, significant appearance changes between enrollment and live operation cause cosine similarity to drop below the confirmation threshold, producing a **false Unknown** result.

### 3.2 Triggers for False Unknowns

| Appearance Change | Approximate Embedding Shift (ΔCosine) |
|---|---|
| New prescription glasses | 0.08 – 0.18 |
| New hair colour / style | 0.05 – 0.15 |
| Beard growth (3–4 weeks) | 0.10 – 0.20 |
| Weight change (significant) | 0.12 – 0.22 |
| Dramatic makeup | 0.10 – 0.25 |

Approximate shifts are derived from internal experimentation; individual variation applies.

### 3.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Implement **online gallery update**: upon confirmed identity, re-compute and store the latest embedding, weighted by an exponential moving average.
- Support **multi-shot enrollment** (3–5 images from different lighting conditions and expressions) and store the per-class embedding centroid.
- Provide a semester-start re-enrollment workflow via the admin dashboard.

## 4. Demographic Bias in Training Data

### 4.1 ArcFace MS1MV2 Training Set

ArcFace was trained on the **MS1MV2** dataset, a cleaned subset of MS-Celeb-1M containing approximately 5.8 million images of ~85,000 identities. The composition of MS1MV2 skews heavily toward:

- **East Asian** identities (~35 %)
- **Caucasian/European** identities (~40 %)
- South Asian, African, Latin American, and other demographics are underrepresented.

### 4.2 Implications for South Asian / Indian Deployment

This system is intended for deployment in an Indian educational institution. The training data distribution mismatch may manifest as:

- Higher **False Reject Rate (FRR)** for underrepresented demographic groups.
- Reduced cosine margin separation for individuals from populations not well-represented in MS1MV2.
- **Equal Error Rate (EER) drift**: published ArcFace EER figures (≈ 0.3 % on LFW) may not generalise to local student populations.

### 4.3 Evaluation Gap

This project has **not** conducted a demographic subgroup analysis on a representative local dataset. Such an evaluation requires a locally collected, IRB-approved dataset, which falls outside the scope of this academic prototype.

### 4.4 Mitigation Recommendations

- Fine-tune ArcFace on a locally collected, consented South Asian face dataset.
- Conduct bias audits (EER per demographic group) before any production deployment.
- Consult published fairness benchmarks (e.g., DiveFace, BUPT-Balancedface) during model selection.

## 5. Lighting and Environmental Conditions

### 5.1 Degradation Scenarios

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Extreme back-lighting (window behind subject) | Face under-exposed; SCRFD confidence drops |
| High-intensity IR illumination (some security lights) | Sensor saturation; detection failure |
| Fluorescent flicker (older tube lights, 50/60 Hz) | Frame-level exposure variation; quality gate rejects |
| Direct sunlight glare | Specular highlights on forehead; liveness false-negative |
| Very low ambient light (< 50 lux) | Low SNR; alignment landmark inaccuracy |

### 5.2 Quality Gate Coverage

The `QualityGate` module partially mitigates these effects by rejecting frames with:
- Laplacian variance below `min_laplacian` (blur / low-contrast)
- Mean brightness outside `[min_brightness, max_brightness]`

However, frames that narrowly pass quality thresholds under poor lighting can still produce degraded embeddings that fail matching.

### 5.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Deploy the camera with controlled, diffuse front-lighting (e.g., LED ring light at 5000 K colour temperature).
- Add a per-frame exposure histogram check and adaptive threshold scaling.
- Consider a near-IR camera with dedicated 850 nm illumination for consistent conditions.

## 6. Single-Angle Enrollment

### 6.1 Behaviour

The enrollment workflow captures a **single frontal image** per student. ArcFace was trained with augmented views, and its backbone provides moderate pose robustness; however, embeddings of profile or three-quarter views can differ substantially from the frontal enrollment embedding.

### 6.2 Approximate Recognition Rates by Pose

| Yaw Angle | Approximate Recognition Rate (ArcFace) |
|---|---|
| 0°–15° (near-frontal) | ≥ 95 % |
| 15°–30° (slight turn) | 85–93 % |
| 30°–45° (three-quarter) | 70–82 % |
| > 45° (profile) | 40–65 % |

The `QualityGate` rejects frames where estimated yaw exceeds `max_yaw_deg` (default 30°), which prevents the worst-case profile matches from reaching the matcher—but also means students who turn their heads during the scan window will not be recognised.

### 6.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Collect 3–5 enrollment images at different yaw and pitch angles.
- Average the embeddings or store a per-angle gallery (multi-view gallery matching).

## 7. Identical Twins and Lookalikes

### 7.1 Behaviour

ArcFace and virtually all face recognition systems rely on subtle facial geometry differences that are substantially reduced in **monozygotic (identical) twins**. Studies have reported inter-twin cosine distances on ArcFace embeddings that fall within the intra-class variation range, rendering the two individuals indistinguishable at standard operating thresholds.

### 7.2 Risk Assessment

For typical university class sizes, the probability of having enrolled monozygotic twins in the same section is low but non-zero. If such a case occurs, one twin could inadvertently mark attendance for the other.

### 7.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Flag enrollments where any two students exhibit cosine distance below `2 × threshold` and alert the administrator.
- For confirmed twin pairs, require an additional biometric (e.g., fingerprint, student ID card scan) or rely on manual attendance.

## 8. Database Scale and Matching Complexity

### 8.1 Current Implementation

The `Matcher` module implements **linear nearest-neighbour search** over all enrolled embeddings using NumPy:

```python
# Pseudocode — actual implementation
distances = 1 - (embeddings_matrix @ query_vector)  # cosine distance, O(N)
```

This approach loads all embeddings into RAM at startup and performs an exhaustive search on each inference cycle.

### 8.2 Scalability Analysis

| Enrolled Students (N) | Approx. Search Time (CPU) | RAM Usage |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | < 0.1 ms | ~1 MB |
| 200 | ~0.3 ms | ~4 MB |
| 500 | ~0.8 ms | ~10 MB |
| 2 000 | ~3.5 ms | ~40 MB |
| 10 000 | ~18 ms | ~200 MB |

For the intended deployment scale (50–300 students per institution), linear search is acceptable. Beyond approximately **500 students**, an **Approximate Nearest Neighbour (ANN)** index (e.g., FAISS `IndexFlatIP`, HNSW) is recommended to maintain sub-millisecond search latency.

### 8.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Replace NumPy linear search with `faiss.IndexFlatIP` (exact) or `faiss.IndexHNSWFlat` (approximate) for institutions with > 500 enrolled students.
- Partition the gallery by course/section to reduce effective N per query.

## 9. CPU-Only Inference Performance

### 9.1 Performance Profile

The system is designed to run on commodity hardware without a discrete GPU. Measured performance on a mid-range CPU (Intel Core i5-11th Gen, 4 cores):

| Component | Latency (CPU) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SCRFD face detection | ~25 ms / frame | 640 × 480 input |
| Landmark-based alignment | ~1 ms | NumPy affine transform |
| Quality gate | ~2 ms | Laplacian + histogram |
| MiniFASNet liveness | ~15 ms | Single face crop |
| ArcFace embedding | **~180 ms** | Bottleneck; ONNX Runtime |
| Matching (N=200) | < 1 ms | NumPy |
| **Total inference pipeline** | **~220–240 ms** | **≈ 4–5 FPS throughput** |

### 9.2 Display vs. Inference FPS

The `DisplayThread` reads from `SharedState` and renders to an OpenCV window at up to **25 FPS**, independently of the inference pipeline. Users see smooth video even though recognition results update only at ~5 FPS.

### 9.3 Implications

A 5 FPS recognition throughput means that for a student walking through the camera's field of view in 2 seconds, only ~10 inference cycles are available. Combined with the quality gate filtering, effective confirmed identifications per pass may be only 3–6 votes in the `TrackVoter` majority window.

### 9.4 Mitigation Recommendations

- Use GPU-accelerated ONNX Runtime (`CUDAExecutionProvider`) to reduce ArcFace inference to ~10–15 ms, raising throughput to ~25 FPS.
- Alternatively, deploy quantised INT8 ArcFace (via ONNX quantisation tools) which achieves ~80–100 ms on CPU.
- Use batch inference when multiple faces are detected in the same frame.

## 10. Privacy and Data Re-identification Risk

### 10.1 Data Stored

| Data Type | Storage Location | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Student name, ID, section | SQLite `students` table | Plaintext |
| Enrollment thumbnail | SQLite `embeddings` table | JPEG bytes (cropped face, ~10 KB) |
| ArcFace embedding vector | SQLite `embeddings` table | 512-dimensional float32 vector |
| Session attendance records | SQLite `attendance` table | student_id + timestamp |

### 10.2 Embedding Irreversibility

ArcFace embeddings are **not directly invertible** to face images; the mapping from pixel space to 512-D embedding space is a many-to-one function with no known closed-form inverse. However:

- **Re-identification risk** exists: if an adversary has access to the embedding database and can query a face recognition API (or run ArcFace locally), they can compare probe images against stored embeddings to determine whether a person is enrolled in the system.
- **Embedding leakage** would allow cross-system re-identification if the same ArcFace model is used in multiple databases.

### 10.3 Thumbnail Concern

The stored 112 × 112 JPEG thumbnail is a recognisable face image. If the database file is exfiltrated, thumbnails directly expose students' biometric data.

### 10.4 Mitigation Recommendations

- **Encrypt the SQLite database** at rest using SQLCipher or AES-encrypted file systems.
- **Delete thumbnails** after enrollment confirmation; retain only embeddings.
- **Hash or encrypt embeddings** using cancelable biometrics techniques (e.g., random projection with a secret key).
- Restrict database file permissions to the application service account.

## 11. DPDP Act Compliance (India)

### 11.1 Regulatory Context

The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)** governs the processing of personal data in India. Face images and biometric embeddings constitute **sensitive personal data** under the Act.

### 11.2 Applicable Obligations

| Obligation | Current System Status |
|---|---|
| **Consent** (Section 6) | ⚠ No explicit in-app consent mechanism; must be obtained at institution level before enrollment |
| **Purpose limitation** (Section 4) | ✓ Data used solely for attendance; stated in enrollment flow |
| **Data minimisation** | ⚠ Thumbnails retained beyond necessity |
| **Storage limitation** (Section 8) | ⚠ No automatic data retention / deletion policy implemented |
| **Data security** (Section 8) | ⚠ Database not encrypted at rest |
| **Grievance redressal** (Section 13) | ✗ No in-system mechanism; must be provided by institution |
| **Data Fiduciary registration** | ✗ Required if the institution qualifies as a Significant Data Fiduciary |

### 11.3 Recommendations for Production Deployment

1. Obtain written informed consent from students (or guardians for minors) before biometric enrollment.
2. Publish a Privacy Notice describing data types collected, retention period, and contact for data requests.
3. Implement a data deletion endpoint: students or administrators can request removal of all stored biometric data for a given student ID.
4. Encrypt the database and restrict access via role-based controls.
5. Consult the institution's Data Protection Officer before production deployment.

> **Disclaimer:** The above is not legal advice. Consult a qualified legal professional for DPDP Act compliance.

## 12. Adversarial Robustness

### 12.1 Absence of Testing

This project has **not** conducted any adversarial robustness evaluation. No tests have been performed against:

- **Physical adversarial patches** (e.g., Sharif et al. eyeglass-frame attack on face recognition)
- **Digital adversarial perturbations** applied to live video frames
- **Model extraction / query attacks** against the matching module
- **Backdoor / data poisoning** during enrollment

### 12.2 Implications

An adversary with knowledge of the ArcFace model weights (which are publicly available) could craft adversarial perturbations—imperceptible to humans—that cause the system to misidentify them as an enrolled student or to evade recognition entirely.

### 12.3 Mitigation Recommendations

- Conduct adversarial robustness testing using libraries such as `foolbox` or `ART` (Adversarial Robustness Toolbox).
- Apply randomised input preprocessing (e.g., JPEG compression, random resizing) as an inference-time defence.
- Treat the system as a **supplementary tool** (not sole authority) for attendance in security-sensitive contexts.
